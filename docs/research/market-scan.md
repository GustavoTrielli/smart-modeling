# Market scan: tools that already turn garment ideas into 3D and patterns

Research for the ticket "Market scan: tools that already turn garment ideas into 3D and patterns". Prices, terms, license files and repository activity were checked on **2026-09-26**. Facts link to the source that owns them; my judgments are labelled **Assessment**; anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified**.

## Short answer

- **Nothing we can build on covers the whole flow.** The closest products are closed: Style3D Studio's AI Garment (text or image in, pattern pieces plus a 3D garment out) is Enterprise-only, and PatternFast, the company behind Tailornova, sells image-to-pattern by subscription. Neither lets us embed it in our app.
- **Commercial 3D and CAD suites are checking tools, not building blocks.** CLO, Browzwear and Style3D handle the 3D preview, drafting, grading and DXF export well, but their APIs run inside a licensed desktop app, and CLO's terms allow plug-ins only for the licensee's own business.
- **Most AI fashion startups make images, sometimes with tech packs, but no sewing patterns:** The New Black, Raspberry AI, Vizcom, Mercer (formerly CALA), Audaces LooksMaker and Style3D AI. A few claim real pattern pieces (Style3D AI Garment, PatternFast, FashionINSTA, Sewrio, Myaiart); none of those claims has been independently checked.
- **The reusable parts are open source, one step each:** GarmentCode's pygarment library (MIT) and GarmentCodeData (CC BY 4.0) for machine-generated pattern programs; FreeSewing (MIT) for made-to-measure womenswear drafting with seam allowances; Valentina (GPL-3.0) for DXF-AAMA, DXF-ASTM, PLT and tiled PDF export; ezdxf (MIT) for a DXF writer of our own.
- **Research output is made for simulation, not cutting.** GarmentCode has no seam allowances and GarmentCodeData uses minimal stretch, so knit-aware, production-ready rules must be ours, checked by the pattern maker.
- **License traps:** GarmentCode's simulator fork is non-commercial (upstream NVIDIA Warp is now Apache-2.0); SewFormer and DressCode have no license; GarmageNet is CC BY-NC-ND; ChatGarment's and Design2GarmentCode's weights have no stated license.
- **In Brazil, Audaces is the CAD to stay compatible with** (it claims more than 20,000 companies as users), so our pattern files must open cleanly in it. Molde.me is a browser-based local alternative.

## Our flow and a few terms

| Step | What it means here |
|---|---|
| 1. Idea | Turning words (Portuguese or English) and optional reference images into something a drafting engine can use |
| 2. 3D preview | Draping the pattern on a virtual body to judge and adjust the design |
| 3. Pattern | Drafting the flat pieces in the base size |
| 4. Grading | Producing the pattern in every size of the size chart |
| 5. Export | Writing the pattern file: DXF-AAMA or DXF-ASTM for apparel CAD, PLT or PDF for plotting |

- **Parametric pattern**: drawn by formulas from body measurements, so changing a measurement redraws the pieces.
- **Block** (base): a basic fitted pattern, such as a bodice or skirt block, that designs are derived from.
- **Made-to-measure** (sob medida): drafted for one person's measurements instead of graded across a size chart.
- **Seam allowance** (margem de costura), **notch** (pique), **grain line** (fio): the margin outside the stitching line, and the marks that show how pieces line up and which way the fabric runs.
- **Grade rules** (regras de gradação): per-point offsets that move a base-size pattern to each other size. **Marker making** (encaixe): nesting all pieces across the fabric width before cutting.
- **DXF-AAMA / DXF-ASTM**: DXF files following the apparel conventions (AAMA, and the later ASTM D6673) for piece names, grain lines, notches and sizes. **PLT** files hold HPGL, the plotter language.

License shorthand for ADR-0001: **permissive** (MIT, Apache-2.0, CC BY 4.0) allows commercial use with credit. **GPL-3.0** allows commercial use; distributing a modified GPL program means sharing its source ([FSF](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#GPLCommercially)), but running it on our own server is not distribution ([FSF](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#UnreleasedMods)), and separate programs shipped together stay separately licensed ([FSF](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#MereAggregation)). **Non-commercial** fails ADR-0001, and so does **no license file**, since then "no one may reproduce, distribute, or create derivative works" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository)).

Price tags: **Free**; **Low-cost** is under about US$100 a month for one user; **Expensive** is more, or quote-only.

**CLO.** Individual is [$50 a month](https://www.clo3d.com/en/plans/individual); yearly billing saves 25% and there is a 14-day trial ([plans](https://www.clo3d.com/en/plans)). It imports and exports DXF-AAMA and DXF-ASTM, one graded size group per file ([help](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export)); grades with size groups and RUL grade-rule files ([help](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015578947-Add-Apply-Grading)); builds avatars from your own measurements, bust, underbust and cup included ([help](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/33854094900633-Adjust-Avatar-Size)); and has knit tools ([help](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/51521137972121-Knit-Swatch-Editor)). CLO 2026.0 (April 13, 2026) added "Sketch on Avatar" and "Pattern Drafter with Auto Grading" ([notice](https://www.clo3d.com/en/resources/notices/1374)). Its AI Studio makes textures, graphics, renders and images, not patterns ([help](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/49988591822873-AI-Studio)); a third-party claim of CLO text-to-garment is not backed by CLO's 2026.0 notice or its AI Studio documentation. The API is a C++ plug-in SDK plus Python inside the app ([docs](https://developer.clo3d.com/), [Python](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014409267-How-to-use-Python-Script-in-CLO)), at V11.0.0 in August 2026 ([changelog](https://developer.clo3d.com/changelog.html)). The [Additional Terms](https://legal.clo-set.com/additional-clo) allow plug-ins only for your "Internal Business Needs", forbid providing them to "any third party" or syndicating access, name a "Headless API" without details, and exclude commercial use during trials. **Assessment:** CLO cannot power a web app we might sell, but a trial can check that our DXF files import cleanly.

**Marvelous Designer** (same company) costs [$39 a month](https://www.marvelousdesigner.com/en/pricing) and "can be used for commercial purposes" by freelancers. Its help center says it lacks what physical production needs, "to import/export DXF, to print out the paper patterns" ([help](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358309052313--Can-I-use-Marvelous-Designer-for-physical-garment-production)); it has a [Python API](https://developer.marvelousdesigner.com/). **Assessment:** not useful for patterns.

**Style3D Studio** (Style3D, Hangzhou). Plans are Free (non-commercial), Individual at $40 a month or $380 a year, and Enterprise and Academic on quote; "AI features are currently available only in the Enterprise plan" ([plans](https://studio.style3d.com/)). The plan table lists markers, grading, notches, DXF import and DXF and PLT export; which tier includes which export was not readable (unverified). **AI Garment** "supports generating garment designs from given text/images, analyzing pattern pieces and sewing relationships based on the design, and automatically constructing 3D digital garments", outputs "2D patterns and a 3D garment model with sewing relationships", offers these womenswear garments: dress, pants, shorts, jumpsuit, shirt, tank top, T-shirt, hoodie, jacket and robe (no skirt), and needs staff to activate a trial ([help](https://help.style3d.com/studio/en/c4905/c2c8/7cd2/b61b)). Studio has in-app [Python scripting](https://help.style3d.com/studio/en/c4905/c2c8/38ae/3ad4); the Style3D Simulator SDK is an Unreal Engine plug-in for games and film ([site](https://simulator.style3d.com/), [SDK help](https://help.style3d.com/simulator/en/5f16)); [style3d.ai](https://www.style3d.ai/) makes fashion images. **Assessment:** the closest commercial match to our flow, but Enterprise-only and a black box; nobody has shown its patterns are production-ready.

**Browzwear.** Freelancer costs $750, FreelancerPlus $1,500 and Teams $3,950 a year; Enterprise is on request ([plans](https://browzwear.com/pricing-plans)). Freelancer plans exclude "Plugins or CAD integrations" and the "Server Engine (headless VStitcher)" ([FAQ](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13623175-freelancer-and-freelancerplus-faq)), "a version of VStitcher without a user interface" licensed through sales ([help](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13065059-open-platform-and-vstitcher-server-engine)). Plug-ins can be written in [Python](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13066097-creating-python-plugin). VStitcher imports and exports DXF ([help](https://help.browzwear.com/hc/en-us/articles/4921557292953-Importing-Exporting-and-Opening-Files)), and ASTM DXF gained rotation support in 2026.1.2, released April 30, 2026 ([blog](https://browzwear.com/blog/vstitcher-2026.1.2-whats-new)). "Most avatars are fully parametric", including Sofia, a plus-size female avatar ([blog](https://browzwear.com/blog/explore-browzwear-avatars)). **Assessment:** automation needs an Enterprise contract, which doesn't fit the budget.

**Tailornova and PatternFast** (Mylinego Inc.). Tailornova turns ready-made parametric designs ("smart templates") into made-to-measure patterns in the browser ([price](https://tailornova.com/price/)): Personal $29 a month ("Personal use ONLY!", PDF); Commercial $49 (commercial and reseller license; unlimited PDF, PLT, DXF and DXF-AAMA; graded size runs as individual sizes); Commercial Pro $79 ("PatternFast AI", automated standard size grading, tech packs, single-size markers); Enterprise on quote. The [designer](https://tailornova.com/designer) sells single patterns for $10; its womenswear menu offers dresses, tops, jumpsuits, pants, skirts, sweatshirts and T-shirts. Patterns redraft when you "choose a type of fabric, stretch factor and fit", offer seam allowance options and sewing instructions, and fit models need "just a few measurements"; that page still calls 3D garment simulation "coming soon" ([features](https://tailornova.com/explore)), though the price page lists a 3D preview. The [terms](https://tailornova.com/terms) keep all content, "sewing patterns" included, as Tailornova's property and forbid derivative works unless a plan allows them. Y Combinator lists the company as PatternFast (formerly Tailornova/Couturme, Winter 2019, 23 people) with custom API integration ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/tailornova-couturme)). PatternFast offers "Concept-to-pattern: upload visual ideas and generate AI-powered flat patterns" ([site](https://patternfast.com/)) at early-adopter prices of $49 and $79 a month ([pricing](https://patternfast.com/pricing)). **Assessment:** the closest product to our v1, minus the chat: a benchmark and possible stopgap, nothing we can reuse.

**Audaces** (Florianópolis). Audaces360 includes Moldes (pattern making with a grading table, DXF import and export, automatic seam allowances, notches and grain lines), Audaces 3D, Encaixe (marker making), Digiflash (digitizing paper patterns from a photo) and plotters ([home](https://audaces.com/pt-br), [Moldes](https://audaces.com/pt-br/solucoes/moldes)). Its AI product **LooksMaker** turns reference images into realistic garment images, preliminary front and back technical drawings and a first technical description, plus virtual try-on, but no patterns ([LooksMaker](https://audaces.com/pt-br/solucoes/looksmaker)). LooksMaker Essential costs R$1,499 and Advanced R$4,499 a month, with mandatory onboarding ([plans](https://audaces.com/pt-br/planos/empresas)); a student plan at R$89 a month or R$899 a year bundles LooksMaker credits and a basic Moldes ([student plan](https://audaces.com/pt-br/planos/estudantes)); business pattern CAD is quoted. Audaces claims more than 20,000 companies as users (same plans page). I found no developer API, only [ERP integrations](https://audaces.com/pt-br/integracoes).

**Molde.me** (Brazil) is a browser app for pattern making with automatic grading, AI marker making, photo digitizing and cost reports, claiming more than 1,500 garment factories as customers ([home](https://www.molde.me/)). It exports "four types of plt, plus pdf, dxf, svg, emf and png", offers a 5-day trial, and leaves design to you: "creative and technical control remains with the customer" ([FAQ](https://molde.me/perguntas-frequentes)); its pattern module also lists AAMA ([module](https://www.molde.me/modulo/modelagem)). "From R$99 a month" appears only in a search snippet (unverified).

**Others.** TUKAcad costs $19 a month (Learning Edition, 4 graded sizes per file), $99 or $199 (Professional), with a 14-day trial ([product](https://tukatech.com/products/tukacad)). Lectra [Modaris](https://www.lectra.com/en/fashion/products/modaris) and [Optitex](https://optitex.com/products/2d-and-3d-cad-software/) are industry pattern CAD, priced on request. **Valentina.** GPL-3.0 per its [license file](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/blob/develop/LICENSE_GPL.txt), with commits on 2026-09-26 and v1.1.1 on 2026-09-18 ([project](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina)). The [changelog](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/blob/develop/ChangeLog.txt) records DXF-AAMA and DXF-ASTM export with a CLO compatibility mode, "PLT export format as a container for HPGL", and a September 2026 fix raising piece-name text on ASTM exports to the size ASTM D6673 recommends. It drafts from formulas over "standard sizing tables or an individual's set of measurements" ([project](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina)), has a companion layout app (Puzzle) for laying out pieces, and has a command-line export mode that takes a measurement file, format and size dimensions ([source](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/blob/develop/src/app/valentina/core/vcmdexport.cpp)). It designs nothing itself: a person, or our code, writes the pattern. **Assessment:** the strongest open export engine for production formats, and a free viewer for checking our files. Headless use on a Linux server is untested (unverified).

**Seamly2D.** GPL-3.0 ([license](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/LICENSE)), weekly releases up to v2026.9.21.146 ([releases](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/releases)), and a Brazilian Portuguese translation update among its 2026-09-26 commits. It reads multi-size and individual measurement files ([README](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D)). Its exports include DXF-AAMA and flat DXF, with ASTM commented out and no PLT ([source](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/src/libs/vwidgets/export_format_combobox.cpp)), and it has a command-line export mode ([source](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/src/app/seamly2d/core/vcmdexport.cpp)).

**FreeSewing.** MIT ([license](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/src/branch/develop/LICENSE)). It moved from GitHub, now archived ([GitHub](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing)), to [Codeberg](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing) in April 2025; `@freesewing/core` 4.10.2 was published on 2026-09-13 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@freesewing/core)). Of its 80 [designs](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/src/branch/develop/designs) (tags from each design's `about.json`), the womenswear-relevant ones include the Bella, Breanna and Noble bodice blocks, the Sarah skirt and Titan trouser blocks; Penelope pencil, Sandy circle and Sunny split skirts; Sasha skater and Sophie slip dresses; Teagan, Tina and Toni tops (tagged for stretch fabric) and the Simone button-down; Charlie chinos, Waralee wrap pants and Lumina and Lumira leggings (stretch). A plug-in handles bust adjustments. The editor exports SVG and tiled or untiled PDF, with optional seam allowance and no DXF ([editor docs](https://freesewing.eu/docs/editor/)). Patterns are drafted from one person's measurements, not graded. **Assessment:** the most production-minded open drafting code for womenswear, in JavaScript that runs in a browser. Grading would mean drafting each size from the size chart, and DXF would need our own writer.

**ezdxf** is MIT and active ([repo](https://github.com/mozman/ezdxf)); the pattern file formats research covers whether it can write compliant AAMA or ASTM files.

For womenswear, knit and plus sizes, the GarmentCode-based models (ChatGarment, AIpparel, Design2GarmentCode) inherit GarmentCode's coverage: tops, skirts, pants, dresses and jumpsuits, a simulation fabric model with no knit ease, and the body shapes of its datasets. How far those bodies reach into plus sizes was not checked (unverified).

- **GarmentCode** is MIT ([license](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode/blob/main/LICENSE)) and installs with `pip install pygarment` ([install](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode/blob/main/docs/Installation.md)); its programs are in [assets/garment_programs](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode/tree/main/assets/garment_programs), and there is a free [online configurator](https://garmentcode.ethz.ch/). Simulation needs the authors' Warp fork, whose license says it "only may be used or intended for use non-commercially", meaning "for research or evaluation purposes only" ([license](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/NvidiaWarp-GarmentCode/blob/main/LICENSE.md)); upstream NVIDIA Warp is Apache-2.0 ([license](https://github.com/NVIDIA/warp/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). The bundled SMPL-based bodies are "SMPL-Body" meshes under CC BY 4.0 ([readme](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode/blob/main/assets/bodies/Readme.md), [SMPL-Body license](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/bodylicense.html)), but the full SMPL shape model is "for non-commercial scientific research purposes", with commercial licensing through Meshcapade ([SMPL model license](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)). Other bodies come from [GarmentMeasurements](https://github.com/mbotsch/GarmentMeasurements) (GPL-3.0 code), built from the European CAESAR scans ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17609)). A code search found no seam-allowance handling. **Assessment:** its patterns are made for simulation, not cutting.
- **GarmentCodeData** covers "tops, shirts, dresses, jumpsuits, skirts, pants" with three materials whose stiffness is fixed "to have minimal stretching" ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17609)). ETH's record says "Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International" for v2 and v1; I read it from a 2025-06-24 [archived copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250624210229/https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/handle/20.500.11850/690432) because the live repository blocked automated access ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3929/ethz-b-000690432)).
- **ChatGarment** is Apache-2.0 ([repo](https://github.com/biansy000/ChatGarment)), built on LLaVA and LISA, and uses [GarmentCodeRC](https://github.com/biansy000/GarmentCodeRC) and [ContourCraft-CG](https://github.com/biansy000/ContourCraft-CG), both MIT. Its weights sit on a university SharePoint with no license statement ([install](https://github.com/biansy000/ChatGarment/blob/main/docs/Installation.md)), and the [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sy000/ChatGarmentDataset) has no license tag.
- **AIpparel** code is MIT ([repo](https://github.com/georgeNakayama/AIpparel-Code)); weights and GCD-MM annotations are tagged MIT on base model `llava-hf/llava-1.5-7b-hf` ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/georgeNakayama/AIpparel)). LLaVA's notice says checkpoints follow the base model's license, such as the Llama 2 community license, and its data follows OpenAI's terms ([LLaVA](https://github.com/haotian-liu/LLaVA#readme)).
- **Design2GarmentCode** is MIT ([repo](https://github.com/Style3D/design2garmentcode-impl)). It calls GPT-4o by default and uses Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct ([Apache-2.0](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct)) plus fine-tuned weights on Google Drive with no license statement; its optional simulation uses the non-commercial Warp fork.
- **GarmageNet** is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 ([license](https://github.com/Style3D/garmagenet-impl/blob/main/LICENSE)). **SewFormer** ([repo](https://github.com/sail-sg/sewformer)) and **DressCode** ([repo](https://github.com/IHe-KaiI/DressCode)) have no license file. **SwiftTailor** (CVPR 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19053)) and **GarmentWeaver** (August 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30550)) had no public code that a GitHub search could find.
- **Assessment:** the research on "AI routes from words and images to sewing patterns" should compare these models' quality and hardware needs; this scan only checks whether we may use them.

## 4. AI fashion startups: images versus real patterns

In AI marketing, "pattern" often means a print, not a sewing pattern: PatternedAI makes seamless textile prints ([site](https://www.patterned.ai/)), and CGDream's "sewing pattern generator" makes images that only look like patterns ([page](https://cgdream.ai/features/free-ai-sewing-pattern-generator)).

**Images only, no sewing patterns:**

| Tool | What it outputs | Price |

## 7. Recommendations (assessment)

1. **Build the flow around an open parametric pattern engine** instead of waiting for a product to license. For "Choose the pattern-generation route and its AI model", the realistic options are a language model writing parameters or code for GarmentCode's MIT library (the route Design2GarmentCode and ChatGarment demonstrate), or FreeSewing-style drafting code, which is closer to production (blocks, seam allowances, stretch options) but has a narrower design space. A hybrid, where a language model picks options for our own womenswear drafting rules, may combine the two.
2. **Treat production-readiness as our own work.** No research model outputs the seam allowances, notches, knit ease or plus-size proportions a factory accepts; those rules belong in our drafting code and the pattern maker's review.
3. **Start export with Valentina's command line** as a separate program, so GPL obligations stay with Valentina. Switch to an ezdxf writer if headless use or compliance falls short.
4. **Do not use GarmentCode's simulator** for the 3D preview; point the cloth simulator research at upstream NVIDIA Warp and ContourCraft.
5. **Benchmark before deciding, for a few dollars:** Tailornova's free designer (then one $10 pattern, or a $49 month for DXF-AAMA and PLT), the GarmentCode configurator and Style3D's Free plan. Having the pattern maker review one Tailornova pattern would calibrate what "production-ready" looks like.
6. **Exclude** SewFormer, DressCode, GarmageNet and the GarmentCode Warp fork. Ask the ChatGarment and Design2GarmentCode authors to license their weights only if those models win in the AI routes research.
